The Rules

The urn holds balls in an unknown mix of orange and white. You draw one ball at a time — look at the colour, then drop it back in. Use what you've seen to estimate the chance of drawing white.

An estimate is just a point. The interval tells you how much you should trust it.

The Range Around Your Guess

Saying 0.6 sounds precise — but with 5 draws, that number could easily be off by a lot. A confidence interval puts a bracket around the estimate: wider means less data, narrower means more.

Game 4

Proto-UCB Algorithm

No guessing, no tuning — just always pick the urn that could be best.

Optimistic by Default

Proto-UCB never explores randomly. It's optimistic: it adds uncertainty to your estimate and picks whichever urn looks best under that generous assumption. Unknown urns look great — so it explores them first, naturally.

Early on: few draws → wide intervals → every urn looks promising.
Later: intervals shrink → the real winner pulls ahead. No ε, no schedule, no tuning.

Why It Works

Empty urns start at UCB = 1.0 — the highest possible score. So the algorithm is forced to try everything at least once before settling. The more you draw, the faster the real winner separates from the pack.

Vs. Epsilon-Greedy: you'd have to pick ε upfront and hope it's right. UCB adapts — more exploration early, more exploitation late — without you touching a thing.
