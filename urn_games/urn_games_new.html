The Urn Games

Four games. Hidden urns. Every draw tells you something — if you're paying attention.

The Setup

Each urn holds balls of two colours: white (1) and orange (0). The true mix is hidden. Your job is to figure it out — and then use that knowledge to win.

The Rules

The urn holds balls in an unknown mix of orange and white. You draw one ball at a time — look at the colour, then drop it back in. Use what you've seen to estimate the chance of drawing white.

💡 Every draw is independent: the ball goes back in, the urn stays the same. You never run out — you just get more evidence.

The Shadow Urn Formula

Your formula breaks down at the extremes: if every draw so far was white, it returns 1.0 — total certainty, from just a handful of draws. The shadow urn fixes this by pretending you started with one extra white ball and one extra orange ball already drawn.

(1 + white_count) / (n + 2)

It nudges every estimate away from 0 and 1 — the less data you have, the bigger the nudge.
With 1000 draws it barely changes anything; with 3 draws it can make a real difference.

Game 2

Confidence Intervals

An estimate is just a point. The interval tells you how much you should trust it.

The Range Around Your Guess

Saying 0.6 sounds precise — but with 5 draws, that number could easily be off by a lot.
A confidence interval puts a bracket around the estimate: less data mean wider, more mean narrower.

The Confidence Interval Formula

💡 Why 1/√n and not 1/n?
Doubling your samples doesn't halve the uncertainty — it only shrinks it by about 30%, because √2 ≈ 1.4.
Randomness is stubborn.

Exercise · Build the Interval

Compute the margin using the formula above. Remember: √n is n ** 0.5 in Python.
def compute_confidence_interval(sample):
if len(sample) == 0:
return (0.0, 1.0)
p_hat = frequentist_estimation(sample)
n = len(sample)
margin =
lower = max(0.0, p_hat - margin)
upper = min(1.0, p_hat + margin)
return (lower, upper)

In Python, n ** 0.5 is √n. So 1.0 / n ** 0.5 gives 1/√n. This is your margin.

Proto-UCB Algorithm

UCB stands for Upper Confidence Bound — always pick the option whose upper estimate is highest. Proto because this is a simplified version of the full algorithm.

Optimistic by Default

Proto-UCB never explores randomly. It's optimistic: it adds uncertainty to your estimate and picks whichever urn looks best under that generous assumption. Unknown urns look great — so it explores them first, naturally.

Remember the confidence interval formula from Game 2? That's exactly what we're using here as our "optimism":
